The 48-Hour Work Order Close Standard

By Daniel Looper HEDS October 26, 2026 3-minute read

Walk into almost any heavy equipment shop on a Monday morning and ask to see the open work order report sorted by age.

You will usually find tens of thousands of dollars in finished wrench work sitting in the 31 to 60 day bucket waiting on a warranty claim, a parts transfer, or a service writer to finish the story. Once the tech walks away from the machine, the sense of urgency disappears.

HEDS Floor Standard
Every work order closes within 48 hours after final labor is rendered.
≤48h Work Order Close
15 min Daily WIP Stand-Up
95-100% Labor Utilization Target
≤85% Red Flag Threshold

How to Make the 48-Hour Standard Stick

Our standard on the shop floor is simple. Every work order closes within 48 hours after final labor is rendered.

To make that stick, the service manager runs a 15-minute WIP stand-up every morning. You start with the oldest open work order on the sheet, put one name next to it, and get a firm close date before moving to the next job. Zero-dollar work orders get scrubbed with the GM or controller the same day.

Why Delayed Closing Damages Labor Utilization

When you let finished jobs sit open for weeks, your Labor Utilization (hours invoiced divided by hours on work orders) drops toward the 85% red-flag line because hours get written down just to clear old paperwork. Close the ticket within 48 hours while the job is fresh and your target stays at 95% to 100%.
