How to Manage WIP in a Heavy Equipment Service Department

How much money is sitting in your shop right now in work orders that are 60, 90, or 120 days old? Pull your open WO report. Sort by oldest open date. Count the dollars in each aging bucket. If you have WOs older than 90 days with no named owner and no close date, you are carrying write-offs that have not been written off yet.

WIP (Work-in-Process) is the total dollars sitting in open work orders: parts, labor, and sublet costs that have not been billed. Every dollar in WIP is a dollar of operating cash tied up in unfinished work. The longer it sits, the less likely it ever gets billed at full value.

The WIP Aging Bucket System

Every work order falls into an aging bucket based on how long it has been open. The buckets tell you exactly where the problem is and what to do about it.

BucketStatusAction
0 to 14 daysNormalNo action needed. Standard repair cycle.
15 to 30 daysAgingReview in the weekly WIP meeting. Identify the bottleneck.
31 to 60 daysAction requiredNamed owner assigned. Hold reason documented. Target close date set.
60 to 89 daysEscalateService manager review. Decision: close this week or kill the WO.
90+ daysWrite-off candidateGM review. Most of these will never be billed at full value.

HEDS target: total WIP should not exceed 10 to 15 days of average monthly service sales. WIP over 30 days should be under 5% of total WIP. Days to Invoice (DTI) should be 3 to 5 business days from completion to invoice.

The 48-Hour Close Standard

After final labor is rendered on a work order, the WO closes within 48 hours. The 48-hour clock starts when labor is complete, not when parts arrive.

If the WO cannot close within 48 hours, it gets three things:

  1. A named owner. One person responsible for closing it. Not "the service department." A name.
  2. A specific hold reason. Parts on backorder with no alternate sourced. Quote sent to customer with no follow-up. Advisor moved to the next job and forgot this one. The reason matters because the fix is different for each.
  3. A target close date. When will this WO close? If there is no date, there is no plan.

Every WO over 30 days needs all three, or it needs to be killed. A work order open for more than 30 days with no owner, no reason, and no close date is a write-off you have not recognized yet.

The Daily 10-Minute Standup

The standup is where WIP discipline gets installed. It takes 10 minutes. If it takes 30, it is a meeting, not a standup.

The agenda is fixed:

  1. Oldest WO first. Start with the oldest open work order on the board. Who owns it? What is the hold reason? When does it close?
  2. Capacity stress flag. Scheduled hours divided by available hours. If scheduled exceeds available by more than 15%, you have a bottleneck arriving in 2 weeks. Deal with it now.
  3. Top 3 priorities. The three WOs that must close today. Name them. Commit to them.

One decision minimum. If no decision is needed, the standup is not needed. The purpose is rhythm, not duration.

Floor Rule: Oldest-to-newest close priority. 48-hour close standard. Daily 10-minute standup. No exceptions. If your standup takes 30 minutes, cut it to 10. If there is nothing to discuss, the standup is 3 minutes and everyone goes to work.

Total WIP is $174,000. Their average monthly service sales are $340,000. That is 15.3 days of sales, right at the upper limit of the 10 to 15 day target. But $49,000 sits in the 31+ day buckets. That is 28% of total WIP, well above the 5% target.

The 90+ bucket has $6,000 in 4 WOs. The oldest is 127 days. The hold reason: "waiting on customer approval." Nobody has called the customer in 60 days. That WO is a write-off candidate.

The service manager installs the daily standup. Two weeks later, the 31 to 60 bucket drops from $28,000 to $14,000. The 90+ bucket does not move much because those 4 WOs get killed or closed at partial value. But the 0 to 14 bucket tightens because the 48-hour close standard stops new WOs from aging into the problem buckets.

The Three Causes of WO Aging

Every WO over 30 days has one of three root causes. The fix is different for each.

1. Parts on backorder with no alternate

The parts manager sources an alternate or updates the customer with a new ETA. The technician moves to the next job. The WO is paused with a named restart date. No technician sits idle waiting for one part.

2. Quote sent to customer with no follow-up

The service advisor calls the customer within 48 hours. No email-only quotes. If the customer does not respond in 48 hours, the advisor calls. If no response in 7 days, the service manager calls. The quote does not sit in the system aging.

3. The advisor moved to the next job

The advisor forgot this WO exists. The daily standup catches it because the oldest WO is always reviewed first. The advisor gets the WO back, calls the customer, and commits a close date.
