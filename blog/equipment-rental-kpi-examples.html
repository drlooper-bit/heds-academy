5 KPIs Every Equipment Rental Manager Should Track

Time utilization tells you whether the machine was on rent. Dollar utilization tells you whether it was worth owning. Most rental managers track time utilization because it is the easy number. Your DMS spits it out automatically. But a $50,000 skid steer at 101% dollar utilization and a $240,000 excavator at 99% dollar utilization can both show 77% time utilization. The skid steer earns back its cost in a year. The excavator takes three. Same time utilization, completely different capital efficiency.

If your dashboard shows time utilization and nothing else, you are managing activity, not profitability. Here are the 5 KPIs that tell you whether your fleet is making money.

KPI 1: Dollar Utilization

Formula: Actual Rental Revenue divided by Maximum Potential Revenue (rack rate times 365 days)

HEDS Target: 30% to 50%+ by equipment class

Dollar utilization is the metric your CFO and your bank care about. It measures actual revenue against the maximum the machine could have earned at full rack rate every day of the year. A $50,000 skid steer on rent 280 days at $180/day generates $50,400/year, a 101% dollar utilization. A $240,000 excavator on rent 280 days at $850/day generates $238,000/year, a 99% dollar utilization. Both have the same time utilization (77%) but very different capital efficiency profiles.

Calculate dollar utilization by class, not fleet-wide. If excavators are at 70% dollar utilization and light towers are at 30%, your capital is misallocated. You should be buying more excavators and selling light towers. Most rental managers manage fleet-wide numbers and miss the class-level story.

Escalation trigger: Any class dropping below 20% dollar utilization for two consecutive months triggers a formal fleet mix review with the Branch GM.

KPI 2: Rate Realization

Formula: Actual Billed Rate divided by Rack Rate

HEDS Target: 90% or higher

Rate realization below 90% means your rack rate is a fiction. You publish $850/day for an excavator but actually bill $780. That is $73/day in rate erosion. On 8 excavators over 250 rental days, that is $146,000 per year. A manager's salary given away in unmanaged discounts.

Every discount over 5% requires manager approval. Rate erosion tracking catches sweetheart deals and unmanaged markdowns. The counter staff are not giving away your money maliciously. They are giving it away because nobody told them the rack rate is real.

KPI 3: Time Utilization

Formula: Days on Rent divided by Days Available

HEDS Target: 65% to 75%

Time utilization measures whether the machine was physically on a rental. Below 55% on a class means you own too many of that machine for your market. Above 85% means you are turning away business. Both problems are fixable, but only if you measure by class, not fleet-wide.

Time utilization is the starting point, not the ending point. It tells you the machine was busy. It does not tell you whether it was profitable. That is what dollar utilization and rate realization are for.

KPI 4: PM Compliance Rate

Formula: Completed PMs divided by Scheduled PMs

HEDS Target: 90% or higher on-time

PM compliance below 90% means you are choosing breakdowns over scheduled maintenance. The math is simple: planned maintenance costs less than unplanned breakdowns. A PM skipped to save 2 hours of shop time becomes a 3-day field breakdown that costs $2,400 in lost rental revenue plus $1,800 in emergency repair.

Maintenance cost as a percentage of Original Equipment Cost (OEC) should run 3.5% to 5.5%. Over 6.5% triggers a mandatory GM review before another dollar of labor is authorized. The planned-to-unplanned ratio should be 80% planned or higher. If your unplanned ratio is above 20%, your PM schedule is not being followed.

KPI 5: Damage Recovery Rate

Formula: Customer-Paid Damage Costs divided by Total Damage Incidents

HEDS Target: 85% or higher

Equipment rental damage is inevitable. Customers run machines hard in rough conditions with varying skill levels. The question is whether you capture the cost. A 50% recovery rate means you are eating half the damage. The difference between 50% and 85% on a branch with $30,000/month in damage events is $10,500/month. That is $126,000 per year in recovered revenue.

HEDS installs a 4-photo standard on every dispatch and every return. Joint customer and dealer sign-off at dispatch and at return. Past-due collections escalation. Contract compliance: 100% signed contract, certificate of insurance on file, and damage waiver attached. No exceptions.

Why Most Rental Managers Only Track Time Utilization

Time utilization is the easy number. Your DMS generates it automatically. It looks good on a dashboard. 77% time utilization sounds like your fleet is working hard.

Dollar utilization requires actual revenue divided by maximum potential revenue, calculated by class. Rate realization requires tracking every discount and every negotiated rate. Damage recovery requires a disciplined dispatch and return inspection process with photo documentation. PM compliance requires a PM schedule that actually matches your fleet maintenance plan.

These are harder to calculate. But they are the numbers that tell you whether the fleet is profitable, not just busy.

Floor Rule: Time utilization tells you the machine was on rent. Dollar utilization tells you it was worth owning. Rate realization tells you whether your rack rate is real. If your dashboard shows time utilization and nothing else, you are managing activity, not profitability.
