Leading vs Lagging KPIs: Why Most Heavy Equipment Dealers Drive by the Rearview Mirror

By the time your absorption rate, ELR, and WIP aging numbers move, the money is already lost. Titan Machinery reported equipment revenue down 9.2% year over year in their most recent quarter. Dealers reading lagging KPIs found out weeks after the damage was done. The dealers who caught it early were watching leading indicators.

A manager who only reads lagging indicators is driving by looking in the rearview mirror. You see where you have been. You do not see the wall you are about to hit.

Lagging KPIs: What Happened

Lagging KPIs measure the past. They are accurate. They are verifiable. They are also weeks late.

Every one of these is important. Every one tells you something real. But every one is a post-mortem. The loss has already happened. The WO that aged to 90 days is already a write-off candidate. The ELR gap has already cost you $32/hour for a month. The absorption miss has already left the dealership uncovered.

The Capacity Stress Flag

This is the single most valuable leading indicator for a service department. It compares your scheduled hours against your available technician hours, looking 2 to 3 weeks ahead.

Formula: Scheduled Hours divided by Available Hours

If scheduled exceeds available by more than 15%, you have a capacity stress flag. A bottleneck is arriving in 2 weeks. You have two choices: add capacity now (overtime, borrow a tech from another branch, pull in a contractor) or accept that WIP will age and DTI will slip.

Without the capacity stress flag, you find out about the bottleneck when the WIP report shows 20 WOs in the 31 to 60 day bucket. By then, the damage is done. The capacity stress flag gives you 2 weeks to prevent it.

Floor Rule: Lagging indicators show what happened. Leading indicators show what is about to happen. A manager who reads only lagging indicators finds out about the problem weeks after the money is gone.

The Branch Control Score

The Branch Control Score is a daily dashboard of 5 to 7 leading or diagnostic metrics. Maximum 7. Every metric has one named owner with one review cadence. It is visible in the office, updated daily.

Metric selection rule: Every metric on the Branch Control Score must be leading or diagnostic. Not lagging vanity. "Revenue last month" is lagging vanity. "Quote-to-close trend this week" is leading. "WIP aging right now" is diagnostic. "Customer satisfaction score from last quarter" is lagging vanity. "Capacity stress flag for next 2 weeks" is leading.

Examples of what goes on the Branch Control Score:

  • 2-week capacity forecast (leading) — scheduled vs available hours
  • Quote-to-close trend (leading) — quotes sent vs quotes won this week
  • Parts backorder alert (leading) — open backorders affecting scheduled WOs
  • WIP over 30 days (diagnostic) — current dollars in the problem bucket
  • DTI running average (diagnostic) — days to invoice this week
  • Unbilled labor dollars (diagnostic) — month-to-date labor not yet invoiced

Every metric has one owner. The accountability cascade rule: if the metric moves the wrong direction, the owner explains why at the daily standup and commits a fix date. No metric without an owner. No owner without a review cadence.

The Daily Standup

The Branch Control Score gets reviewed at the daily standup. Ten minutes. Three things happen, in order:

  1. Scorecard scan. Any metric in the red zone gets a 30-second explanation from its owner. No explanation, no owner, no fix date — pick one before the standup ends.
  2. Capacity stress flag. The 2-week forecast. Is a bottleneck coming? What is the plan?
  3. Top 3 priorities. The three things that must close today. Named. Committed.

If the standup does not include the 2-week capacity forecast, it is a status meeting. If it takes 30 minutes, cut it to 10. The purpose is rhythm, not duration.

The Industry Signal

Titan Machinery, the world's largest Case and New Holland dealer, reported Q2 fiscal 2027 revenue of $496.4 million, down 9.2% from $546.4 million a year earlier. Equipment revenue was $328.5 million, down from $376.3 million. Agriculture revenue fell 10.3%. Europe revenue declined 32.6%.

Dealers who caught this signal early were watching leading indicators: quote volume dropping, quote-to-close rates slipping, parts backorder trends shifting, capacity stress flags calming (which sounds good but means your service department is about to get quiet and absorption is at risk). Dealers who caught it late were reading their month-end absorption rate weeks after the equipment slowdown started.

The difference between catching it early and catching it late is 4 to 6 weeks of runway. In 4 to 6 weeks, you can adjust staffing, defer non-essential spending, push service campaigns, and protect absorption. Or you can find out at month-end that absorption dropped 5 points and the dealership is uncovered.
