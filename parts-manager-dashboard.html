Module 1: Understanding Your Department's Financials
ERP Source: Parts Dept P&L (CDK)
The Diagnosis

Parts departments consistently operate without understanding the four foundational financial KPIs that determine whether the department is generating profit or draining it. When gross margin falls below 33%, parts absorption drops below 30% of the location's absorption, or salary exceeds 30% of gross profit, the department becomes a cost center rather than a profit center. You cannot fix what you do not measure.

KPI 1: Gross Margin
Target 33-37%
Pure profit % on sales after parts cost
KPI 2: Absorption Rate
Target 30% to 40%
Parts' contribution to the location's absorption
KPI 3: Parts Share of Gross Margin
Target 25-30%
Parts gross margin dollars as a % of total dealership gross margin
KPI 4: Salary / GP %
Target ≤ 30%
Parts payroll consumed per dollar of GP
Worked Example: KPI 1 (Parts Gross Margin)
BENCHMARK: 33-37%
Gross Margin % = [ (Parts Revenue - Parts Cost of Goods Sold) / Parts Revenue ] × 100
1. Parts Revenue$420,000
2. Parts COGS$270,000
3. Gross Profit$150,000
4. Margin Result35.7% (PASS)
What It Measures
Pricing matrix discipline, purchasing efficiency, avoiding emergency freight erosions, discount oversight.
What It Does NOT Measure
Inventory efficiency. You can have a 35% margin and still have $200,000 in dead stock tying up cash.
Worked Example: KPI 2 (Parts Absorption Rate)
BENCHMARK: 30% to 40% of the location's absorption
Parts contribution % = [ Parts Gross Profit / Location Total Operating Expenses ] × 100
1. Parts Gross Profit$150,000
2. Location Operating Expenses$250,000
3. Parts contribution60.0% (above the 30% to 40% target)
Worked Example: KPI 3 (Parts Share of Dealership Gross Margin)
Parts Share % = [ Parts Gross Margin $150,000 / Total Dealership Gross Margin $600,000 ] × 100 = 25.0% (PASS)

Target: 25-30% of total dealership gross margin. Protects dealership from equipment market downturns.

Worked Example: KPI 4 (Salary % of GP)
Salary % = [ $40,000 / $150,000 ] × 100 = 26.7% (PASS)

Target: 30% or less. Team generates $3.75 GP for every $1.00 in payroll.

PRO TIP
You cannot manage a parts department from a P&L statement alone. The four foundational KPIs tell you whether the department is profitable, self-sustaining, properly weighted, and efficiently staffed. If any one of the four is outside the target range, the department is leaking profit, and the leak will not fix itself.

Day 2: Inventory Discipline & Leaks | Diagnostic Matrix
Module 3: Finding the Leaks: What Is Broken and Why

Parts departments lose profit through specific, identifiable leaks, not through general "bad management." Every leak has a root cause on the floor, and every root cause has a dollar amount attached to it. You cannot fix a percentage. You can only fix a behavior.

Leak Fix Prioritization Rule

Fix the leak with the highest dollar impact and simplest behavior change first.

Simple (7 days)
Pricing overrides, matrix markup enforcement, emergency order approvals.
Moderate (14 to 30 days)
Min/max recalculation, stock order schedules, RTV purge batching.
Hard (30+ days)
Service department shop-sourcing protocols, wholesale contract renegotiations.
PRO TIP
You cannot fix a percentage. You can only fix a behavior. 'Gross margin is 28%' is not a problem. 'Counter staff is discounting 15% of all retail tickets without manager approval' is a problem you can fix. Name the behavior, name the owner, name the date.

Day 3: Floor Execution & Forecasting | Inventory Lifecycle
Module 5: Floor Execution: Inventory Discipline (KPIs 5 to 10)

The second half of the KPI set measures inventory discipline, how efficiently you buy, stock, and deploy parts. These six KPIs separate a well-run parts department from a warehouse full of dust and tied-up capital.

KPI 5: Gross Turns
Annual COS / Avg Monthly Inv
$1.8M / $500K = 3.6x (Target: 3-4x)

3-4x is the sweet spot. Below 3x: too much stock (capital sleeping on shelves). Above 4x: generally too little stock (fill rate risk). Either side is a warning.

KPI 6: Stock Order Efficiency
[ Stock Receipts / Total Receipts ] × 100
$75K / $100K = 75.0% (Target: 80% or higher. This example grades WARN.)

Stock orders are planned & discounted. Every emergency order is a freight penalty.

KPI 7: Obsolescence & Excess
[ Dead Stock Value / Total Inv ] × 100
$50K / $500K = 10.0% (Target: <5%)

Dead capital. Execute quarterly purge: RTV to vendor, markdown, or scrap.

KPI 8: Parts-to-Labor Ratio
Shop Parts $ / Total Work Order Labor $ (customer, warranty and internal)

Work orders only (no counter sales). One dealer-wide ratio, not split by cat class.

$120K / $100K = $1.20:1 (Target: $1:$1, PASS $1.00 to $1.50)

Service shop partnership. The classic rule of thumb is $1 of parts sales for every $1 of labor sales on work orders, which supports profit in both departments. Below $1:1 indicates missed parts quotes or external buying. Well above $1:1, check that all labor is being billed.

KPI 9: First-Time Fill Rate
[ Line Items Filled on First Request / Total Line Items Requested ] × 100
Counter: 855 / 950 = 90.0% (Target: ≥ 85%). Service: 900 / 950 = 94.7% (Target: ≥ 90%, no upper limit)

Customer & technician availability metric, tracked separately for the counter and for service. Service fill below 90% causes shop downtime. Counted by line item, then averaged.

KPI 10: Days of Supply
Inventory Value / Daily Usage
Set per dealer, with 90 days or less as the HEDS starting point. Over 90 days grades WARN.

Velocity tiering is a general rule of thumb, not a standard: about 30 days on fast-movers (A), 90 days medium (B), 180 days slow (C). The parts manager still has to research days of supply part by part, because not everything fits a bucket. A part you know you will need that the manufacturer cannot supply stays on hand whatever its tier. Each dealer sets its own figure, with 90 days or less as the HEDS starting point.

PRO TIP
Inventory discipline is not about having less inventory, it is about having the right inventory. The six execution KPIs tell you whether your buying is efficient, your stock is healthy, your depth is correct, and your coverage matches actual demand.
