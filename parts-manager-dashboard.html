Module 1: Understanding Your Department's Financials

ERP SOURCE: Parts Dept P&L (CDK)
// THE DIAGNOSIS

Parts departments consistently operate without understanding the four foundational financial KPIs that determine whether the department is generating profit or draining it. When gross margin falls below 30%, absorption drops below 60%, or salary exceeds 27.5% of gross profit, the department becomes a cost center rather than a profit center. You cannot fix what you do not measure.

KPI 1 // GROSS MARGIN
Target 30–35%
Pure profit % on sales after parts cost
KPI 2 // ABSORPTION RATE
Target ≥ 60%
% of total store expenses covered by parts GP
KPI 3 // SALES MIX %
Target 25–30%
Parts revenue as % of total store sales
KPI 4 // SALARY / GP %
Target ≤ 27.5%
Parts payroll consumed per dollar of GP
// WORKED EXAMPLE: KPI 1 (PARTS GROSS MARGIN) BENCHMARK: 30–35%
Gross Margin % = [ (Parts Revenue − Parts Cost of Goods Sold) / Parts Revenue ] × 100
1. PARTS REVENUE$500,000
2. PARTS COGS$350,000
3. GROSS PROFIT$150,000
4. MARGIN RESULT30.0% (PASS)
What It Measures: Pricing matrix discipline, purchasing efficiency, avoiding emergency freight erosions, discount oversight.
What It Does NOT Measure: Inventory efficiency. You can have a 35% margin and still have $200,000 in dead stock tying up cash.
// WORKED EXAMPLE: KPI 2 (PARTS ABSORPTION RATE) BENCHMARK: ≥ 60.0%
Absorption % = [ Parts Gross Profit / Total Fixed + Variable Expenses ] × 100
1. PARTS GROSS PROFIT$150,000
2. TOTAL STORE EXPENSES$250,000
3. ABSORPTION RATE60.0% (PASS)
// WORKED EXAMPLE: KPI 3 (SALES MIX %)
Parts Sales % = [ $200,000 / $800,000 ] × 100 = 25.0% (PASS)

Target: 25–30%. Protects dealership from equipment market downturns.

// WORKED EXAMPLE: KPI 4 (SALARY % OF GP)
Salary % = [ $40,000 / $150,000 ] × 100 = 26.7% (PASS)

Target: ≤ 27.5%. Team generates $3.74 GP for every $1.00 in payroll.

HEDS FLOOR RULE // P&L REALITY

“You cannot manage a parts department from a P&L statement alone. The four foundational KPIs tell you whether the department is profitable, self-sustaining, properly weighted, and efficiently staffed. If any one of the four is outside the target range, the department is leaking profit — and the leak will not fix itself.”

Module 3: Finding the Leaks — What Is Broken and Why

DIAGNOSTIC MATRIX

Parts departments lose profit through specific, identifiable leaks — not through general "bad management." Every leak has a root cause on the floor, and every root cause has a dollar amount attached to it. You cannot fix a percentage. You can only fix a behavior.

// LEAK FIX PRIORITIZATION RULE
Fix the leak with the highest dollar impact and simplest behavior change first.
SIMPLE (7 DAYS): Pricing overrides, matrix markup enforcement, emergency order approvals.
MODERATE (14–30 DAYS): Min/max recalculation, stock order schedules, RTV purge batching.
HARD (30+ DAYS): Service department shop-sourcing protocols, wholesale contract renegotiations.
HEDS FLOOR RULE // BEHAVIORAL SPECIFICITY

“You cannot fix a percentage. You can only fix a behavior. 'Gross margin is 28%' is not a problem. 'Counter staff is discounting 15% of all retail tickets without manager approval' is a problem you can fix. Name the behavior, name the owner, name the date.”

Module 5: Floor Execution — Inventory Discipline (KPIs 5–10)

INVENTORY LIFECYCLE

The second half of the KPI set measures inventory discipline — how efficiently you buy, stock, and deploy parts. These six KPIs separate a well-run parts department from a warehouse full of dust and tied-up capital.

KPI 5 // GROSS TURNS
Annual COS / Avg Monthly Inv
$1.8M / $500K = 3.6x (Target: 3–4x)

Below 3x: capital sleeping on shelves. Above 5x: understocked with fill rate risk.

KPI 6 // STOCK ORDER EFFICIENCY
[ Stock Receipts / Total Receipts ] × 100
$75K / $100K = 75.0% (Target: >75%)

Stock orders are planned & discounted. Every emergency order is a freight penalty.

KPI 7 // OBSOLESCENCE & EXCESS
[ Dead Stock Value / Total Inv ] × 100
$50K / $500K = 10.0% (Target: <10%)

Dead capital. Execute quarterly purge: RTV to vendor, markdown, or scrap.

KPI 8 // PARTS-TO-LABOR RATIO
Shop Parts $ / Customer Labor $
$120K / $100K = $1.20:1 (Target: $1–$2:1)

Service shop partnership. Below $1:1 indicates missed parts quotes or external buying.

KPI 9 // FIRST-TIME FILL RATE
[ Fills on First Request / Total Requests ] × 100
855 / 950 = 90.0% (Target: ≥90%)

Customer & technician availability metric. Below 90% causes shop downtime.

KPI 10 // DAYS OF SUPPLY
Inventory Value / Daily Usage
Coverage: 30 / 90 / 180 Days

Velocity tiering: 30 days on fast-movers (A), 90 days medium (B), 180 days slow (C).

HEDS FLOOR RULE // RIGHT INVENTORY

“Inventory discipline is not about having less inventory — it is about having the right inventory. The six execution KPIs tell you whether your buying is efficient, your stock is healthy, your depth is correct, and your coverage matches actual demand.”
