Due Diligence & Operational Inspections

We walk your floor, pull your ERP reports, and show you exactly where the cash is leaving. This is not a desktop review. It is a full onsite operational audit of your service, parts, and rental departments.

What We Audit

Service Department

HEDS reviews Last Labor to Invoice (work orders still open more than 48 hours after the last labor), labor recovery and unbilled hours, effective labor rate against posted rate, quote follow-up and close rate, warranty recovery rate, first-time fix rate, and absorption contribution.

Parts Department

HEDS reviews fill rate and backorder exposure, dead stock and aging inventory, the stock order to emergency order ratio, gross profit margin on parts, bin accuracy and receiving discipline, physical inventory procedures, and master data cleanliness in ERP.

Rental Fleet

HEDS reviews time utilization rate, dollar utilization rate, off-rent turnaround time, downtime percentage, maintenance cost as % of OEC, PM status by unit, and rent-ready status accuracy.

Branch Operations

HEDS reviews absorption rate, which is (Service GP + Parts GP) ÷ Total Operating Expenses of the Whole Location. The location target is 100% to 110%. It also reviews cross-functional accountability, daily operating rhythms, scorecard discipline and review cadence, working capital tied up in WIP and inventory, EBITDA quality and normalized margin, and branch-to-branch consistency.

What You Get

The audit delivers an operational findings report with dollar-quantified revenue leaks, a department-by-department scorecard showing current state against industry benchmarks, and a prioritized fix list ranked by dollar impact and ease of implementation.

It also delivers a Last Labor to Invoice analysis of work orders open past 48 hours after the last labor, with specific hold reasons and dollar exposure, a labor reconciliation showing unbilled hours and dollar exposure, a parts inventory health snapshot covering fill rate, dead stock and aging, and a rental fleet utilization and downtime analysis. Buy-Side Guidance From Product History

Every machine leaves a record in its service history, parts purchases, downtime and maintenance cost. This is how HEDS reads that record.

Start by collecting the unit's service work orders, parts purchases, down days and total maintenance cost for the period you want to judge. Work out maintenance cost as a percent of the unit's OEC, and compare it with the rest of your fleet and with the HEDS target in the table above.

Step One: The Industry Knowledge Assessment

Every HEDS training cycle starts with the proctored 50-question Industry Knowledge Assessment. It shows HEDS where each student's knowledge is strong and where it is thin, so the five days focus where that manager needs them most. It is included in the 5-day course and is not sold on its own.

Why industry knowledge matters

A manager who does not know how Labor Recovery and Labor Utilization work cannot see paid technician hours that never reach a work order, or hours worked that never reach an invoice. Those hours are paid for every week whether anyone sees them or not.

A manager who does not know why work orders must close within 48 hours of the last labor lets finished jobs sit open. That is a miss on Last Labor to Invoice, and it holds revenue and cash in jobs that are already done.

A manager who cannot explain absorption cannot tell whether the department's gross profit covers the location's operating expenses. Each of these gaps costs money for as long as it goes unseen.

The score report shapes the five days

Each question maps to a KPI used across the heavy equipment industry, so the assessment tests industry knowledge and not the HEDS curriculum. The score report gives scores by category against the HEDS targets, an overall score from 0 to 100, and three focus areas to close first. HEDS uses it to decide where to spend the time with each student during the 5 days.

Why proctored

An unproctored online test measures how well someone uses Google and ChatGPT. A proctored test, with no open books and no AI assistants, measures what the manager actually knows when the DMS is down and the customer is on hold. Proctored means the score is real, so the gaps it shows are real.
